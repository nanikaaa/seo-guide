1. Simple explanation

Each definition links to its source: Google's own documentation where it exists, otherwise WordPress, web.dev, MDN or a Japanese marketing glossary.

What SEO means

SEO (search engine optimization) is the work of helping search engines such as Google find our pages, understand what they are about, and show them to people who search for what we offer. It also means making the page useful once someone arrives. SEO cannot guarantee a ranking. Google decides rankings; we can only remove problems and make our pages clear and helpful.

Source: Google: SEO Starter Guide · Google: Do you need an SEO? (no one can guarantee a ranking)

What MEO means

MEO (map engine optimization) is a term used mainly in Japan. It means improving how a business appears in Google Maps and in the map results of Google Search. Most of this work happens in the Google Business Profile (the company's listing on Google Maps), not on the website. Google says local results depend mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that a complete, accurate profile helps. The website supports MEO by showing the same company name, address and phone number as the profile.

Source: シナジーマーケティング 用語集「MEO」 · makeshop 用語集「MEO」 · Google Business Profile: Tips to improve your local ranking (relevance, distance, prominence) · Google Business Profile: Guidelines for representing your business

How each area contributes

  • Design: a clear layout, readable text on phones, and obvious buttons help visitors stay and contact us. Design also decides where important text appears (as real text, not inside images).
  • Coding: correct HTML (one main heading, real links, alt text on images), fast loading, pages that work on phones, and templates that actually display each page's content.
  • Content: what we say about our services, our works, our news and our company. Google rewards content written for people, from real experience. Coders write the SEO text (titles, descriptions, alt text); the real facts about the company come from management.
  • Website settings: site title, language, which pages may appear in Google (indexing), the sitemap, redirects, and HTTPS. We have the project's WordPress admin accounts on the development and production servers, and Yoast SEO is installed, so we set these in WordPress admin.

Source: Google: Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content · Google: Mobile-first indexing · Google: Core Web Vitals

Who handles what

  • Coders handle: HTML structure, templates, links, image sizing and compression, page titles, meta descriptions, alt text, keyword and topic choices for each page, Yoast SEO settings in WordPress admin (indexing, sitemap, Open Graph, structured data) on development and production, the 404 page, and technical checks with evidence.
  • We ask management or the media team only for real information: company name, address, phone, email and hours, the official Instagram account, real photos and works, permission to show testimonials, the privacy policy text, and the Google Business Profile.
  • Real information already in the design: where the approved design already shows real text or details (for example text inside an image), we use it as it is, including for alt text, and do not ask again.
  • Management decides: target audience and services, the official company details, who owns the Google Business Profile, and access to Search Console and analytics.

Technical terms used in this plan

Index / indexing
Google's database of pages. A page must be indexed before it can appear in search results. Source: Google: How Search works
Crawl
When Google's program (Googlebot) visits and reads a page by following links. Source: Google: How Search works
noindex
An instruction in a page that tells Google not to show that page in search results. Source: Google: Block indexing with noindex
robots.txt
A small file at the site root that tells crawlers which paths they may visit. It does not reliably keep a page out of Google; noindex or a password does that. Source: Google: Introduction to robots.txt
Sitemap
A list of the site's page URLs that helps Google find them. WordPress creates one at /wp-sitemap.xml. Source: Google: Sitemaps overview · WordPress: XML sitemaps in WordPress 5.5
Canonical URL
The one preferred address for a page when the same content can be reached at several addresses. Source: Google: Consolidating duplicate URLs
Title and meta description
The title is the page name shown in the browser tab and often as the blue link in Google. The meta description is a short summary Google may show under it. Source: Google: Title links · Google: Snippets and meta descriptions
Alt text
A text description of an image, used by screen readers and by Google to understand the image. Source: Google: Image SEO best practices · MDN: img alt attribute
Structured data
Extra code (usually JSON-LD) that describes facts such as company name, logo and address in a format Google can read. Source: Google: Introduction to structured data
Core Web Vitals / LCP / CLS
Google's loading and stability measurements. LCP is how long the largest visible item takes to appear; CLS is how much the layout jumps while loading. Source: Google: Core Web Vitals · web.dev: LCP · web.dev: CLS
Staging
A test copy of the website. It must never appear in Google. Source: Google: Control what you share (password protection, noindex)
NAP
Name, Address, Phone. These must match exactly between the website and the Google Business Profile. Source: Google Business Profile: Guidelines for representing your business
